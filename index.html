LINER NOTES // 喫茶論

The Art of the Unhurried Midnight

"A kissaten is not a place to buy fuel for productivity. It is a shelter where the master brews one cup at a time, and the record plays from opening track to locked groove."

In the 1970s, as Tokyo hurried through its postwar industrial miracle, subterranean jazz kissaten stood stubbornly still. They were dimly lit sanctuaries where writers, night-shifters, and jazz purists gathered not to chatter, but to listen in reverent silence.

At YORU, we uphold this unhurried ritual. Our beans are roasted over Bincho-tan charcoal, rested in stoneware jars, and ground by hand only after you sit down. The flame underneath our glass siphons glows with an amber halo, illuminating the slow ascent of water against darkness.
